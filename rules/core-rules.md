# Dogs of War — Core Rules

**Draft.** Rules are added here once they're decided. The decisions and the reasoning behind them live in [`design/00-foundations.md`](../design/00-foundations.md). Anything not yet written out is marked *to be written*.

## How the rules fit together

Dogs of War is played on the **Infinity N5** rules. Anything this document doesn't mention works exactly as N5 says. This document covers only what Dogs of War changes or adds, and where the two disagree, this document wins.

- **The N5 rules** are on the official [Infinity wiki](https://infinitythewiki.com) (© Corvus Belli). The chapters are linked below, and [n5-index.md](n5-index.md) links every page of every chapter.
- **Weapons, skills, equipment and hacking programs:** every one in the catalogue is listed in [armory.md](armory.md), with a short summary and a link to its official rule.
- **Our own rules** are written in our own words. Rules brought back from the previous edition, N4, are marked **(N4)**.

## 1. The setting

Players command **mercenary companies** in the Infinity universe. The game should feel like Infinity to anyone who has played it.

Our own content never uses faction or unit names. Dogs of War shares Infinity's skills, equipment and weapons under their own names, and adds its own when a specific need calls for it. That includes the alien skills Exrah and Shasvastii, so a company can be alien-coded if its players want.

## 2. Building a company

| | |
|---|---|
| **Allocation** | 200 Requisition for a standard game |
| **Pilot and TAG** | Exactly one pilot and one custom TAG. The pilot is fully custom: a Specialist Operative by default, or a Doctor, Engineer, Hacker, Forward Observer or other specialist instead. The TAG is built from a chassis (S6, S7 or S8), components, and one Signature slot. |
| **Elite Guns** | Up to 5, including any held in reserve. Each is a fixed profile with one optional upgrade: a stat bump, a skill, or an equipment or weapon swap. Command Tokens can raise the limit. A second pilot is an expensive Elite Gun option. |
| **Hired Guns** | Free (0 Requisition) and Irregular. Basic troops with no customization: they share one profile, and templates change only their equipment. Specialist templates carry only a pistol and a CC weapon. |
| **Lieutenant** | Required, and costs Requisition. The pilot and some Elite Guns can take it. NCO costs more than Lieutenant. |
| **On the table** | At most 1 TAG or pilot, plus 10 infantry. A reserve TAG called in by a second pilot is the only way to field a second TAG. |

The catalogue enforces these limits. Prices are *to be written*.

## 3. Round structure

A game lasts **3 rounds**. Each round runs through these phases in order. Each phase matches a unit category in the list, with TAG and Pilot acting together. In each phase, the player with initiative takes a full N5 Active Turn while the opponent reacts with AROs, and then the roles swap. Only the models that belong to the current phase may spend Orders.

1. **Impetuous:** Impetuous troops.
2. **Pilot:** pilots and the TAG they're in, including a second pilot.
3. **Elite Guns.**
4. **Hired Guns.**
5. **Reinforcement:** new arrivals deploy, then act with their own Orders. Arriving isn't an action.

The player with initiative goes first in every phase. The Lieutenant Order can be spent in any phase.

## 4. Orders

- Your pilot and each Elite Gun gives a Regular Order to a shared pool. Any model eligible in the current phase can spend from it.
- Hired Guns are Irregular. Each Hired Gun's own Order is spent only on itself, in the Hired Guns phase.
- A pilot's Tactical Awareness Order is spent in the Pilot phase.
- Pool Orders left unspent carry on into later phases, and Hired Guns can use them in the Hired Guns phase.

**Tracking the pool with tokens:**
1. At the start of the round, take one Order token for each of your Regular models.
2. Discard a token each time you spend a Regular Order.
3. At the start of each of your turns within a phase, discard one unspent token for each Regular model you've lost since your previous turn.

## 5. Command

- **Command Tokens:** the N5 starting pool, slightly enlarged (number *to be written*). They are never regained. On top of everything N5 allows, they pay for drop pods, replacement TAGs, extra Elite Gun slots and calldowns.
- **Suppressive Fire** works exactly as in N5.

## 6. Reinforcements and calldowns

Reinforcements arrive in the Reinforcement phase. Each one uses the first of these methods that's possible:

1. **Drop pod (costs 1 Command Token):** lands anywhere on the table. It scatters and damages whatever it lands on (*to be written*).
2. **Deployment (Tactical):** see below.
3. **Your deployment zone**, if neither of the above is possible.

> **Deployment (Tactical)** — *Dogs of War skill.* Every reinforcing model has it. Place the model within the Zone of Control of an allied model and outside the LoF of every enemy.

- Elite Gun reserves are chosen before the game. Hired Gun reserves are unlimited. The only limit on arrivals is the 10-infantry cap.
- **Replacement TAGs:** during the Pilot phase, a pilot can call in a TAG from the stock lineup, which is the same for every company. It costs Command Tokens, roughly most of the pool. Prices *to be written*.
- **Calldowns:** orbital and aerial support, built on N5's [Speedballs](https://infinitythewiki.com/Request_Speedball). The list is *to be written*.

## 7. Pilots and TAGs

### Mount and Dismount (N4)

N5 replaced crewed TAGs with remote-operated ones and dropped the rules for getting in and out. Dogs of War deliberately breaks from the N5 baseline here and brings back N4's Pilot rule, along with N4's movement rules for mounting:

- **Getting in.** A pilot that ends a skill with the Movement label in base contact with the TAG climbs in at the end of that Order. The same move lets a pilot Jockey an enemy TAG instead.
- **Getting out.** At the start of an Order, right after declaring the Order's first skill with the Movement label (or an Entire Order skill), and before any distance is measured, the pilot gets out. It starts that movement anywhere in base contact with the TAG, wherever its owner chooses, and uses its own profile for the whole Order.
- **Cost.** Neither costs anything extra.
- **Who can.** The movement skill must be one the starting profile could declare. So nobody can get in or out while Engaged or Immobilized, and a TAG can't use Cautious Movement to let its pilot out, because TAGs can't declare it.
- **Markers.** Getting in or out cancels Hidden Deployment and any marker state, on both the pilot and the TAG.
- **An Unconscious TAG.** The pilot can still get out of it. While the pilot stays inside an Unconscious TAG, it gives no Order, and only the TAG counts for scoring, Retreat! and anything else that counts casualties.
- **A Possessed TAG.** Its pilot can't get in or out.
- **While its pilot is out,** the TAG becomes the pilot's [Peripheral (Synchronized)](https://infinitythewiki.com/Peripheral#Peripheral_.28Synchronized.29) and acts on the pilot's Orders. It must stay in coherency with its pilot, or it goes Disconnected until it's back in coherency. *This replaces N4, where an empty TAG couldn't act at all.*
- **Game states carry across.** On getting out, the pilot takes on the TAG's states, except Unconscious and Possessed. On getting in, the TAG takes on the pilot's states, on top of its own.
- **While aboard,** the pilot isn't on the table, so Hacking Programs and E/M can't affect it.
- **While its pilot is in a Null state** (Unconscious, Dead and so on), the TAG is [Pilotless](#pilotless-state) instead. *This replaces N4, which removed the TAG when its pilot died.*

N4 let a pilot board only its own TAG. Dogs of War adds two exceptions, Jockey and Unmanned TAGs, below.

### Pilotless State

*Dogs of War state.*

- **Activation:** the TAG's pilot enters a Null state (Unconscious, Dead and so on).
- **Effects:**
  - Pilotless is **not** a Null state. The TAG stays in play, still counts for scoring, and can be targeted.
  - The TAG can't generate or spend Orders.
  - It can still declare AROs.
  - Its player can spend a Command Token to give it [Total Reaction](https://infinitythewiki.com/Total_Reaction) until Pilotless ends.
- **Cancellation:** a pilot climbs in (the company's second pilot, or an enemy pilot through Unmanned TAGs), or the TAG's own pilot leaves its Null state.

### The rest of the pilot rules

- **Eject:** uses N5's [Transmutation](https://infinitythewiki.com/Transmutation) (Escape System). The profile the TAG turns into is the company's pilot.
- **Jockey:** *Dogs of War rule.* Pilots only. A pilot that ends any movement in base contact with an enemy TAG makes a face-to-face WIP roll against it. *Results to be written.*
- **Unmanned TAGs:** a pilot can climb into an enemy TAG whose pilot has dismounted, and take it over.
- **Building the TAG:** a chassis (S6, S7 or S8), weapons and systems on its mounts, and one Signature slot. Capacity keeps each build coherent. There is no Heat. The catalogue lays out the options.

## 8. Not used from N5

- **Fireteams.** N5's Fireteam rules aren't used, so skills that only work inside a Fireteam (FT Master, Number 2, Strategic Deployment, and the Fireteam parts of others) have no effect. The word "fireteam" may still appear loosely in descriptions.
- **Tri-Core**, an N4 rule the wiki marks as no longer part of N5.

## 9. Table and miniatures

- The table is **36" × 36"**, with very dense terrain.
- Corvus Belli miniatures are recommended, using N5 silhouettes for every model.

## Adding to these rules

- **A new rule:** write it here in our own words, and record the decision and the reasoning in `design/00-foundations.md`.
- **A new weapon, profile or piece of gear:** add it in the catalogue with the NewRecruit Editor. Builds don't touch hand-made entries, unless one has the same name as a wiki weapon. They appear in [armory.md](armory.md) marked †.
- **Beyond Infinity:** new skills, equipment and weapons are fine when they meet a specific need. Give them names that don't clash with N5 terms, and never use faction or unit names.
